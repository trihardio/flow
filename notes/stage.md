# The panels before an app: opening, selections, trading panel, wallets

One area of Flow. What applies everywhere, and the list of the other areas, is in the project's `CLAUDE.md`.

- **Opening screen ("open app panel"):**
  - Logo and a pixel-font "FLOW" (`APP_WORD`; one falling piece per letter) on a grey centre gradient, about 1.5s (`OPEN_HOLD_MS`; 1.7s felt a bit long in the still middle).
  - The wordmark is Press Start 2P at 48px (an 8x8 grid font, so one font pixel = 6px): pure white letters, letter-spacing 6px (one extra font pixel, so no shadow touches the next letter), over a hard shadow offset by one font pixel. The shadow carries a slightly darkened pastel **green** gradient that steps once per pixel row. Keep the size a multiple of 8px so it stays on that grid.
  - Each letter is two stacked spans (`opw-sh` shadow under `opw-fg` text) so the shadow can be gradient-filled; `text-shadow` can't do that.
  - Then the outro: the logo drops off the bottom first, each letter follows at its own moment, speed, drift and spin (either direction). Nothing fades except the backdrop (`.open-panel-bg`): it starts 0.1s after the fall (`OPEN_FADE_DELAY_MS`), takes 0.62s (`OPEN_FADE_MS`, ease-in-out) and is gone just before the first piece leaves the screen. Rassam found 0.45s too fast; keep it unhurried but finished before the pieces exit.
    - The fall is rolled afresh for every play in `openFall` and handed to CSS as `--fd/--ft/--fy/--fx/--fr`; the motion is one transform keyframe (`openFall`) on a slow-to-fast speed ramp (`OPEN_FALL_EASE`, a cubic ease-in in CSS terms). No per-frame JS.
    - Rassam calls this ramp "ease out" (Premiere Pro's term for easing out of a keyframe): slow, then moderately fast, fastest at the end. Never read it as CSS `ease-out`.
    - The wallets are mounted `OPEN_PREP_MS` before the outro (`walletsReady`), hidden behind the still splash. Mounting them on the outro's first frame made the fall stutter.
  - At app start the logo and wordmark are held back (`.open-panel.wait`: in place at 1% opacity, so already drawn) until the fonts are loaded and unpacked and the logo is decoded (`warmFonts`, `openGo`; `OPEN_READY_MAX_MS` at the latest). That is about a tenth of a second, while the window is still opening. Everything after is timed from that moment, so the splash is as long as it was. Before, the 55ms unpacking of the icon font and a late pixel font could land in the middle of the logo coming in (he saw the intro lag "sometimes").
  - While the splash stands still (from `OPEN_WARM_AT_MS` to `OPEN_WARM_CLEAR_MS` before the outro) the **warm-up** runs under it; see below. If its last frame is late, the outro waits for it, `OPEN_WARM_LATE_MS` at most.
  - State: `openPanel` ("in" → "out" → "done"), plus `openRun`, which counts replays.
  - The footer logo (`goHome`) replays it: the splash fades in over the app (`OPEN_COVER_MS`), then `resetToWallets` swaps the app for the selections panel underneath.
- **Selections panel** (between the opening screen and the wallets): a carousel of two on the wallets' own stage (`.launch-wrap.pick`), built to look like the wallets panel: the current-year wallet and a 3D Bitcoin coin. The one in focus is in the middle; the other is tucked to the side (wallet left, coin right) at the side-wallet size and dimness, plus a touch of blur. No labels.
  - State: `choice` (`null` = choosing, `"wallet"` = wallets panel, `"trade"` = on the way into the trading panel) and `pickFocus` (`"wallet"` or `"coin"`, which one is in the middle; adds `.pick-coin`). `resetToWallets` sets them back to `null` / `"wallet"`.
  - Nothing on the stage can be marked as text (`user-select: none` on `.launch-veil`). A second press right behind the first is a double click to the engine, which marked the word under the pointer: the engraved "Rassam" on a card turned green, the selection's colour (he reported it on 6 October 2026).
  - A press on the tucked one, or the scroll wheel, brings it into focus (`focusPick`). A press on the one in focus goes in. Only the one in focus tilts under the pointer.
  - The blur on the tucked one is switched, never animated: `filter` has a 0s transition with a 0.26s delay on the way out and none on the way back.
    - The wallet, the coin and the globe all carry `filter: blur(0px)` at rest, so tucking one away only changes the blur's strength. Gaining or losing a filter makes every layer under it be drawn again; on the wallet that was 25 graphics programs built at once, the few frozen frames of the first wallet ⇄ coin switch after a launch. Never give these a state without a filter. Stills before and after are the same to within 2/255.
  - The wallet is the real wallet unit of the current year (`HOME_WALLET`: the entry of `WALLETS` with `year: 0`), so its cards and expiry are whatever the wallets panel gives that year. Pressing it in focus, cards included, opens the wallets panel around it; the wallets panel itself is unchanged. Esc steps back out: pulled-out card → wallets → selections panel. The hidden arrow in the top-left corner (`.pick-back`, wallets panel only) does the same step; it is invisible until the pointer is in that corner.
  - The coin (`BtcCoin`, `COIN_*`) is deliberately plain, after Rassam's reference (he found beads, circuit traces and a ridged edge "way too detailed"): a wide rim, one thin ring, a big pale symbol standing on darker side walls, and a thick smooth edge. Keep the gold. The edge is `COIN_SIDES` flat strips in a `preserve-3d` box, so the thickness is real geometry; each strip's two shades are worked out once for the resting pose (`COIN_REST`). The faces are SVG: whatever is raised is drawn as a height map and lit (`#cnEmboss`), then laid over the metal as highlight and shadow. No blend modes.
    - Three nested boxes: `.coin-tilt` (pointer tilt, written from JS by `stepCoin`, which also turns the lustre `.cn-lustre`), `.coin-float` (idle float, only while the coin is in focus and not under the pointer), `.coin-3d` (resting pose; flips when pressed).
    - Keep `opacity`, `filter`, `overflow` and blend modes off those three boxes: any of them flattens the 3D. Fades, moves and the blur go on `.coin-unit`.
    - On the wallets panel the coin is `visibility: hidden` once it has faded, so its ~100 layers cost nothing there.
    - Against a hitch the first time the coin comes into focus (he noticed it on first launch): everything the coin is painted on has `will-change: transform`, so it is painted at full size from the start instead of small and then again big; and `.coin-unit` always carries `filter: blur(0px)`, so gaining focus only changes the blur's strength rather than rebuilding how the 3D coin is drawn mid-move.
    - When the wallet is chosen the coin must be gone before the next year's wallet arrives in its place (0.18s fade); coming back, it waits 0.12s for that wallet to leave. Otherwise the two show through each other.
  - Rassam calls the coin-and-globe screen the **trading panel** (5 October 2026). Below it is still written "trading selection"; the tracker behind it is `TradeTracker`.
    - On it the coin faces forward (`.markets .coin-3d`, turning from its resting pose as the panel opens), and the flip lands facing forward.
    - Under the pointer there it does more than on the selections panel (`onCoinMove`, `COIN_STILL`; he asked for the hover 3D to look incredible): it leans up to 29° so the thick edge swings into view, lifts towards the viewer (`translateZ`), a pale light follows the pointer over the face (`.cn-glare`, a plain glow, no blend mode) and the ground shadow slides away under it. All transforms on layers that are already there.
  - Pressing the coin in focus (`openMarkets`) opens the **trading selection** (`choice === "markets"`, `.launch-wrap.markets`): the same carousel of two, now the coin (crypto) and the IDX globe (Indonesian stocks), with `marketFocus` (`"crypto"` / `"idx"`) saying which is in the middle. The wallet slips away to the left and the globe comes in tucked on the right. Esc and the hidden top-left arrow (`.pick-back`) go back to the selections panel with the coin in focus.
  - The one in focus, pressed, goes in (`enterTrade(kind)`, which sets `tradeKind` and `.trade-crypto` / `.trade-idx`); the other fades where it is. Then the stage fades and that trading panel is underneath (`view === "trade"`). Either way that is `TradeTracker`, told which market. Its crocodile goes home like the finance footer's.
    - Crypto: a coin toss on the spot, seen from the front (`coinFlip`: three times end over end about its level axis, quick at first and slowing, with a hop from `coinHop`; it stays anchored in the middle and lands facing forward). Until 5 October 2026 it also turned once round sideways on the way; once the coin faced forward that sent it through slanted poses, which he found very weird. On 6 October 2026 he asked for it to turn on X and Y together, like a coin flipped in real life: it is now one spin about a single axis that leans out of the level one (`rotate3d(1, 0.4, 0, …)`), so both turn at the same rate and it still lands facing forward. The same day he asked for it to be different every time, then on x, y and z. Rolled inside a narrow cone round the level axis (a lean of 0.12–0.7, a little z), every toss still read as the same end-over-end flip, and on 7 October 2026 he said so ("still isn't random, make the x y z rotations random each time"). `enterTrade` then rolled one axis over every direction for the whole toss (2 to 4 turns of it), which was still not what he meant. He spelled it out the same day: "coin 1 spin random rotation, coin 2nd spin another random rotation, coin 3rd spin another… it is not a random rotation that is fixed for all the spins, but each spin is a random rotation angle. make sure all the animation is smooth and not janky". **So a toss is three whole turns, each about an axis rolled for itself** (`coinSpin1` on `.coin-3d`, `coinSpin2` on `.coin-s2`, `coinSpin3` on `.coin-s3`: three boxes one inside the other; `--f1x/y/z/d` … `--f3…`): which way across the screen the turn's axis points (any way round: end over end, sideways like a revolving door, any slant between), how far it leans out of the screen (up to 0.6 either way: the share that turns the coin like a wheel) and which way round it goes. A turn's line is never within 40° of the turn before it, nor the first turn's of the last toss's first (`flipWas`). A whole turn about any axis ends facing forward, so the next sets off another way from there and the toss lands facing forward whatever is rolled.
      - **No seam between the turns**: a turn does not stop before the next begins. Its last 55° or so run out while the next one's first 50° run in, both close to facing forward, where two small turns together are only a lean on a slant in between. Each turn's speed is a straight ramp there, down for the one and up for the other (the figures in the keyframes are that sum worked out; change the times and they must be worked out again). Speed of the toss: about 3.8 turns a second off the thumb, 3.4, 3.3, then slowing evenly to rest; 1.1s in all, as before.
      - **Each turn after the first goes the way that carries on from the one before** (less than a right angle between the two turnings; only the first turn's direction is rolled). Rolled freely, the coin sometimes had to brake almost to a stop and go back the way it came within a tenth of a second: a jolt. Measured frame by frame in the hidden engine: its speed fell from 3.8 turns a second to under 2 and back, a step of 2.1 between two frames; now it never falls below about 2.6 and the biggest step is 0.3 to 0.6.
      - All three are plain transform keyframes, run by the graphics process, so a busy page can't make them stutter. Don't drive the toss from script or from registered numbers.
      - Keep the overlap that short: two turns at rates of their own for the whole toss is what made the slanted poses below.
      - When he asks for random, the difference has to be big enough to see.
    - No scale anywhere in it. Never give X and Y turns of their own at different rates again: that is what made the slanted poses. The lean it had under the pointer is let go of at once on the press (`enterTrade`), with a directional motion blur along the tumble while it is fastest, then the fade after `TRADE_FLIP_MS`. He rejected an even (Gaussian) blur: it must smear up and down like a real motion blur. CSS can only blur evenly, so three plain boxes wrap the coin (`.coin-smear`): the middle one blurs (`coinBlur`), the outer one squeezes it to a quarter along the toss's axis and the inner one stretches it back, which leaves the blur four times weaker along the axis than across it. Both are turned to the axis of the turn that is under way (`--fa1`, `--fa2`, `--fa3`; `coinSmearOut` / `coinSmearIn`), swinging from one to the next while the turns hand over, in step with each other so that together they always come to nothing: the smear follows the tumble whichever way it goes. An SVG blur filter stepped through keyframes was tried and dropped.
    - IDX: the globe spins once round its axis (`IDX_SPIN_MS`), lifts and shrinks a little (`.idx-lift`), and the wordmark "IDX / Indonesia Stock Exchange / Bursa Efek Indonesia" comes up under it, laid out as in the logo. The wordmark shows only then. The fade starts after `IDX_REVEAL_MS`.
  - The IDX globe (`IdxGlobe`, `.idx-unit`): `IDX_BANDS` (the white bands) and `IDX_WORD` (the wordmark) are vector outlines traced from Rassam's picture of the logo, in a 400-unit square that the globe fills; don't redraw them by hand. The red disc, a soft gloss and a ground shadow are CSS.
    - It is a real 3D ball, not a rotated picture (he rejected the flat 2D rotation: "it doesn't feel like a globe"). A small WebGL program (`idxGlobeGL`) draws only the white bands on a ball turned about an axis leaning `IDX_TILT` degrees, so they bunch up at the rim and come round from the back like the earth. At a turn of 0 it is exactly the flat logo, which is why the flat SVG can stand in until WebGL is set up and for good if WebGL is missing. WebGL is set up 0.9s after the trading selection opens (the globe is tucked, small and blurred then, so the swap can't be seen), never earlier: set up just after the opening it collided with the first presses on the selections panel. The context is handed back when the app is entered.
    - **A throwaway WebGL set-up is done at app start** (`warmGL`, 8 October 2026), while the opening's pieces are still held back. The first WebGL set-up of a launch is not small: the graphics process builds its whole WebGL side and the page waits for it, 37 to 134ms in one go, which landed on the floating coin 0.9s after the trading panel first opened. With it done early the globe's own costs about 18ms. The bands' picture is painted once and kept (`idxGlobeGL.pic`).
    - One loop (`stepGlobe`, `globeAnim`) works out the turn and draws: the pointer's pull (it follows the pointer left and right), a slow sway to and fro while it floats idle, and the full spin when pressed. It stops when nothing is moving. The idle bob is CSS (`.idx-float`), paused under the pointer and while tucked away.
- **The card leaves the wallet as one piece, in one move** (5 October 2026; he found the old fade "aesthetically unpleasing" and allowed two assets only if the change is invisible; the first rebuild rose, stopped and set off again, which he found janky, weird and too slow, and it ignored presses until it had finished). The long comment above `.phase2 .wallet-unit.active` in the styles, `CARD_OUT_MS` / `CARD_IN_MS`:
  - One number does all of it: `--cp` (`@property`, 0 in the pocket, 1 out), eased once: 0.5s out, 0.42s back. The wallet drops by it (`translate`), the pocket card cancels that drop and drifts to the middle growing, and the big card makes exactly the same move on top, switched off. Once the card is clear of the pocket (`--ct`, set in `fitCard`) the big card is shown in its place, mid-move; they are the same picture on the same spot at every moment, so it can't be seen.
  - No timers, no stages, nothing locked: a press half-way turns the number round from where it is. Never bring back a state machine or an ignored press here: he reported having to wait seconds before a wallet could be pressed.
  - Another wallet pressed while the card is still going back: the pocket card takes over at once (`.big-card.away`) and finishes inside its wallet as that slides aside. A card pressed while its wallet is still sliding into the middle is handed over late (`cardLate`, `--ct` 0.97), when the two are on the same spot again.
  - `--cp` is only ever written to `translate` and to the cards' own `transform`, never to a property with a transition of its own (the wallet's `transform` has one: each frame would start a new transition).
  - What keeps the hand-over invisible: both faces are one layout (`.slot-in`), the pocket card's corners and shadow are the big card's scaled, the big card's shadow is two layers that cross over (`.card-shadow`, from `--cq`), and for as long as the card is on its way (`flyUntil`) the tilt is let go of and the pointer left alone. The idle float starts once the card is out and is eased out before it goes back.
  - Measured with `tools/perf` journey `pull` (it notes both cards' boxes every frame): at the hand-over they are 0.00–0.04px apart (1.6px in the late case), never a frame with neither, no frame gap over 22ms.
- **Side titles** (`SIDE_WORDS`, `.side-word`; 6 October 2026): "FINANCE" on the wallets panel and "TRADING" on the trading panel, never on the selections panel. Pixel font with real thickness: ten copies of the word set back behind a white face (green for finance, the coin's gold for trading), in the coin's three nested boxes (`.sw-tilt` leans after the pointer, written in `onVeilMove`; `.sw-float` idle float; `.sw-3d` holds the letters). It faces forward (he rejected a turned-aside resting pose the same day): each copy sits a little lower than the one in front, one font pixel in all, so the thickness shows as a lip under the letters head-on, and the sides only show as it sways or leans. He asked for it to share the 3D, floating feel of the wallets and the coin. Opacity only on `.side-word`. The pixel font must be set on the letters themselves (`.sw-l`, `.sw-face`): a `span` there does not inherit it.
- **The wallet follows the year** (`homeWallet`): going home from the finance panels, the selections panel shows the wallet of the year that was on screen and the wallets panel opens on it (home from 2027: the 2027 wallet). A year with no wallet, and app start, give the current year's. Home from a trading panel leaves it as it was.
- **The wallets panel names the year of the wallet in the middle** (14 October 2026, his words: "add the little year texts on wallet panel (2026, 2027, 2028) just like how you did it for the trading apps"): the trading panel's line (`.mk-cap`, see "Polish pass, trading sides" in `notes/style-passes.md`), same place (232px under the middle of the window, about 60px under the wallet), same face and fade, one year per entry of `WALLETS` (this year + its `year`). Three lie on each other and only fade as the wallets step. Shown on the wallets panel only (`choice === "wallet"`), never on the selections panel; it fades while a card is out (`phase` 2: the wallet drops through that line) and at the press that enters. Built, not looked at (his app was open).
- **With Home first** (20 October 2026, `notes/dashboard.md`), entering finance plays the greeting and then fades to Home: the big year's flight into the yearly header is played only with the Classic start screen.
