# Calculators (crypto and stocks)

One area of Flow. What applies everywhere, and the list of the other areas, is in the project's `CLAUDE.md`. These are parts of the trading tracker (`TradeTracker`): read `notes/trading.md` with it. The forex calculator is in `notes/forex.md`.

- **Position calculator** (`PositionCalc`, `calcPosition`): Rassam calls it the main part of the crypto side. Its button (`.calc-fab`) is fixed on the screen: right above the year arrows on the yearly panel, in the bottom-right corner on the monthly one. It opens a pop-up like the chart's.
  - Inputs: coin (always capitals, a grey `/USDT.P` written after it), Long / Short, entry, stop loss, risk $, max leverage, IM $, partial TPs (price and close %, two to start, more with "+ Add partial TP"), full TP. Tab walks the number boxes only; Enter calculates from anywhere in the pop-up (the helper buttons never take the focus). After the first Enter the results follow the levels as they are typed (he trades volatile coins and wants it quick).
  - The risk box is in the main currency: "Risk $", "Risk IDR" or "Risk MYR" (`riskCur` in the inputs). Switching the currency, in the pop-up or on the panel while it was closed, exchanges what is in the box at the rate of the moment (2.5 → 44.750 → 10.2 → 2.5; switching back gives the number he typed); an empty box only changes its label. Rupiah are whole and grouped like the stocks side. `calcPosition` still gets the risk in USDT, and its "Risk $" error wording is renamed on the way out (`say`).
  - Long / Short: neither pressed means the side is read from the levels (the targets decide, the stop settles a tie) and the one it reads lights up faintly with "auto". Pressing one locks it.
  - Levels that can't be right are refused, never worked out: on Enter the results give way to an Error panel that says what is wrong, one line each, and the boxes turn red (stop on the wrong side of the entry, a target on the wrong side or on the entry, a partial that closes 0% or over 100%, partials over 100% together, max leverage under 1x or over 1000x, something missing). A level that is only half typed dims the last good results and gives the reason in one line instead: no red until Enter.
  - Targets typed out of order are taken in the order the price reaches them, and Enter rewrites the boxes in that order. The close % stay with their place (TP1's box is what the first target hit closes).
  - No full TP: the furthest partial stands in for it and closes whatever is left (its own % box is dimmed).
  - Prices take up to 12 decimals (the smallest coins). Something pasted with thousands separators ("$64,250.50") is read as the number it is; a comma typed is a decimal point.
  - "Test levels" (test tools only) fills in a made-up trade on a real-looking coin and calculates it. **Its targets are rolled afresh every press** (`testTargets`, all three calculators; 11 October 2026: "make randomized partial tps, have multiple", "also randomize the % close"): one to five partial targets, never as many as the press before, each closing a share of its own (multiples of 5 that leave the full TP 10% at least). Before, it filled the same two targets and kept whatever stood in their % boxes, so an emptied box came back as "TP1 closes 0%" (the bug he reported). Checked with `tools/perf` journey `testlv` (eight presses on the crypto side: 5, 2, 1, 3, 1, 4, 5, 2 targets, results every time); the stock calculator's button uses the same function and was not pressed.
  - His rule, in `calcPosition`: size = risk / distance to the stop, so the stop always loses the risk. The margin (IM) is $1 whenever it can be: leverage = size, to 2 decimals, never under 1x or over the max. Where the leverage is held, the IM gives instead (size / leverage, 3 decimals). Everything is then worked out again from that rounded leverage and IM, the two numbers he types into the exchange. The stop's real loss stays within 0.05% of the risk.
  - **The IM can be typed** (the "IM $" box under Risk and Max lev, crypto only; 9 October 2026: "make me able to change IM manually (auto input IM too)"). Empty, it is worked out as above and shown in grey in the box with "auto" at its end (1, or what the max leverage forces, e.g. 1.083).
    - **A typed IM never moves the leverage** (11 October 2026: "when changing IM manually dont change the leverage"). Why he types one: Bybit won't take the exact IM the calculator gives, so he ends up $0.20 or $0.30 above it and wants to know what he then loses. The leverage comes from the risk alone; the position is the typed IM at that leverage, and the tiles, the stop's loss, every target and the RR's amounts follow it. The IM is always kept exactly as typed.
    - **Risk and IM go together** (same day: "change the risk automatically if i change IM manualy, and change IM auto when i input risk manual"): whichever of the two is typed, the other is worked out. With an IM typed the Risk box shows what the stop loses, in grey (`riskAuto`, `.pc-in.auto`; no "auto" beside it: the box is 83px wide and the word cut the figure off). Typing a risk empties the IM box, which is worked out again.
    - The risk he typed is kept underneath while an IM is typed (it is what sets the leverage) and is back in its box when the IM box is emptied. So an IM typed with no risk yet is still the "Risk $ is missing" error.
    - The first version (9 October) worked the leverage out from the typed IM so the stop still lost the risk, with red notes when it could not. He did not want that; don't bring it back.
    - Typed as 0 it is an error like any other box. It is always in dollars, whatever the main currency (it is what he types into the exchange), is kept with the other boxes (`im` in the saved inputs) and is a step for undo like any typed box. **Clear levels and Test levels empty it** (it belongs to one position; it used to stay).
    - `calcPosition` takes it as `im` (null: automatic) and hands back what the stop loses as `risk`; `notes` is still handed back, empty. The forex calculator does not pass one.
  - Partial TPs close a share of the whole position each (25% then 30% leaves 45%); a blank one is skipped and the rest move up a number; the full TP closes whatever is left.
  - "Calc result" (after his reference pictures): four tiles (leverage, IM, position size, quantity), Full-position PnL, Partial TP plan, RR. Each amount is in the main currency with the second one in brackets; the second-currency pill appears beside the main one once results are showing.
  - Hidden gear beside the ✕ (invisible until the pointer is in that corner): the exchange rates, to type by hand. Emptying a box goes back to the live rate.
  - The enter button is grey and turns blue under the pointer, as he asked.
  - Tests: `node tools/calc-test.mjs` (124 checks) runs the maths exactly as it stands in `App.jsx`: his pictures, no full TP, a backtest of 15 real-looking trades against an independent "exchange" (quantity bought and sold), ordering, long / short, every error, what gets typed or pasted, extreme inputs, 60,000 random positions, 30,000 random mistakes, and the IM typed by hand (worked examples and 40,000 random positions: the leverage never moves, the position and the stop's loss are IM × leverage). `tools/perf` journey `imbox` (with `--shots`) takes stills of the box empty, typed (the Risk box following), a risk typed after it, and typed as 0. Run it after touching `calcPosition`. One thing it found and fixed: rounding by multiplying drifted on margins in the millions; `roundTo` now uses `toFixed`.
  - A keystroke with results showing costs about 0.4 ms. The pop-up keeps its own state, so typing never re-renders the tracker behind it.
- **Live prices in both calculators** (`useQuote`, `QuoteChip`, `quoteBook`; Mac app only):
  - Prices are asked only while a calculator is open (`useQuote` lives in the pop-ups): he asked for exactly that, and a hidden run counted 0 asks in 10s with it closed, 4 in 6s open, none after closing. The exchange rate is the one other thing fetched: once when the crypto panel opens and on a currency click.
  - The latest price of the coin or stock typed sits at the right end of its box (green dot = live, grey = the remembered one), asked 0.22s after typing pauses and again every 2s (crypto) or 5s (stocks) while the pop-up is open and the window is visible.
  - It is also the grey text in the empty Entry box (and in "Buying at"). Enter takes it for an entry left empty, and a press on the price writes it in. A typed entry is never overwritten.
  - The last 40 prices per side are kept in that side's prefs (`quotes`, with `dataAt`), so offline the last one shows. The calculator's hidden settings say "Updated stock data at Friday 2 Oct, 11:27 PM" (his wording; `stampText`), the source, and "Offline. Using the … data fetched …" when the last ask did not get through.
- **How results arrive, in both calculators** (he asked for it, and for it to be quick so he can keep changing levels):
  - Line by line from the top: every direct child of `.pc-res` / `.pc-err` plays `pcLine` (0.2s, fade and an 8px drop), each `PC_LINE_MS` (12ms) after the one above. `nth-child` gives each its turn (`PC_LINE_CSS`, built once and written into the stylesheet), so nothing is numbered in the JSX. A normal plan is all there about a third of a second after Enter.
  - A line plays once, when it appears. Results already on show stay still while levels are typed; only new rows come in. Pressing Enter on results that are already showing replays nothing.
  - The pop-up eases to its new height (`useEaseHeight` on `.pc-body`, 0.24s) where it used to jump: read after every render, written straight to the element, and a change in the middle of a move carries on from where it is. Nothing is left on the element afterwards. It is the one place `height` is animated on purpose: a box can't be resized with a transform without stretching what is in it.
  - The stock calculator's two pages have a `key` each, so switching page brings the lines in afresh. A target or buy added with the + button (`.pc-line.more`) comes in the same way.
- **Undo / redo in both calculators** (`useCalcUndo`, `CalcUndo`, `calcUndoKey`; 6 October 2026: he asked for the buttons, "small but visible"): two arrows in a box of their own at the left of the helper row, before Clear; ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z do the same.
  - A step is what the boxes held, and whether the results were showing, before a change. Typing on in the same box without a pause of `CALC_UNDO_MS` (0.8s) is one step, so an undo takes back a number, not a digit. Clear, Test levels, + Add, ×, Long / Short, a live price taken and Enter's tidying are each a step of their own. A change that leaves the boxes as they were is none.
  - Every change goes through `update(next, how)`: `"typed"` for typing (`set`, `setTp`, `setBuy`), `"quiet"` for what must not be a step (the risk exchanged on a currency switch, the stock calculator's page switch), anything else a step. Write new boxes and buttons the same way.
  - Undo to a risk kept in another main currency exchanges it into the one of now. The steps last as long as the pop-up is open.
  - A press on the arrows keeps the cursor in the box being typed in (`mousedown` is stopped on their box; a greyed one lets the press through to it).
- **Stock calculator** (`StockCalc`, `calcStock`, `calcAverage`; the same button and pop-up on the IDX/IHSG panel, nothing written after the ticker). Two pages:
  - Position: entry, stop loss, **lots** (he asked for lots in place of a risk amount; the budget box went with it), partial TPs and a full TP as in the crypto one. Beside the lots box is what they come to in rupiah, as they are typed (`.pc-conv`: lots × 100 × the entry, or the latest price while the entry is empty). Stocks are bought in lots of 100 shares with no leverage and no shorting. The results give the buy value, the same with the buy fee, the loss at the stop and the break-even price. Targets sell whole lots. Every figure is net of the broker's buy and sell fee.
  - Average down: the average price and lots held, then further buys (price and lots) give the new average step by step, the money in the stock, the break-even price with fees, and how far the price has to rise from the last buy, before and after. "Aim avg" with "Buying at" answers it the other way round: the fewest whole lots that bring the average down to the aim. He found those two boxes confusing, so a caption above them says what they are for ("Or: how many lots get the average down to…").
  - The Position / Average down switch is `SlideSeg`: its grey box slides to the other choice and takes its width (he asked for it to move, not vanish and come back). Only transforms move: two rounded pieces as wide as the switch, one clipping the other (`.seg-box`, `.seg-fill`); never animate its width. The wrapped's "By item / Single purchases" switch is still the plain `.wr-seg`.
  - Hidden gear: the broker's fees (0.15% buy, 0.25% sell to start). A price that is off its IDX price step and a partial that rounds to no lot are notes under the tiles, not errors.
  - Rupiah boxes group their digits as they are typed (`idrIn`, 8.650); percentages and ratios use a decimal comma like the amounts.
  - Tests: `node tools/idx-calc-test.mjs`: a position and an averaging worked out by hand, a backtest against an independent "broker" (cash out, cash back), 40,000 random positions (the lots as typed, the rupiah they come to) and 20,000 random averagings.
- **The settings panels' status lines** (22 October 2026, the options pass): the position calculator's exchange rates and the stock calculator's broker fees end with labelled lines, each with a dot (`PcStatus`: green live, blue typed by hand, grey otherwise): **Rates** (live and when, typed by hand, or built-in) and **Prices** (`dataNote`). Two plain sentences side by side, "Live rate, fetched …" and "Offline. Using the crypto data fetched …", read as if they said opposite things (one is the rate, the other the coin prices).
