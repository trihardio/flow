He left the placement to me. Save point from before it: `savepoints/before_worth_it.jsx`.

## Where it is
- **Its own pop-up** (`WorthIt`), opened with **⌘B** on the finance side, the round scales button (`balance`) beside Log on the yearly and the monthly panel (`.log-pill.wi-pill`, the yearly pair in `.log-duo`), and on finance's Home toolbar (key `worth`, `go("worth")`). ⌘B or Esc closes it.
- Why not inside Log: Flow's first principle is "seconds to log" ([[PRODUCT]]); a check before buying is a different moment. The two meet in the verdict's **Log it to today**, which files the item as today's expense (the quick log's own `put`).

## How it decides
- **What the thing is**: its words, English and Indonesian, against `WI_TYPES` (≈55 kinds: clothes, shoes, food, coffee, phones, iPhones, AirPods, MacBooks, rides, tickets…), the longest matching word winning and an accessory word winning over the device ("iphone case" is an accessory). No word: the on-device model's category (`flowAI.pick("cat")`), which gives a rough comparison for food and transport (`WI_ROUGH`) and none otherwise. The model can't place arbitrary kinds of thing (zero-shot scored 65%, [[notes/ai]]), so it is only the fallback.
- **The usual price**: low, typical and high for each kind, in rupiah, **Flow's own estimates for Indonesia (2026)**, said so at the foot of the pop-up. Nothing is fetched: PRODUCT.md's offline rule allows two kinds of request and this is neither; a search that day found no free average-price source anyway. Live shop prices would need his OK for a new request.
- **His own figures**: this month's daily limit × its days, less what is spent (what is left), the daily limit, and what he paid before for the same kind of thing (the middle of his last ten, `wiHistory`).
- **The score** (`wiScore`, 0–100): the price against the usual one (100 up to typical, 65 at the high end, falling fast past it), the price as a share of what is left (100 under 5%, 0 at all of it), the cost of each use against the daily limit (how often: Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely, from the kind's usual use; not asked for things used up at once), need 100 or want 40; weighted 40 / 35 / 10 / 15, the price's weight given to the rest when it can't be compared. A want bigger than what is left is 25 at most; a need at a normal price 62 at least; anything over four times the high price 35 (need) or 18 (want) at most.
- **Words**: Sensible (80+, green), Reasonable (60+), Think twice (40+, yellow), Hard to justify (20+, orange), Irresponsible (red). Decide for me calls it a need or a want from the kind.
- **The answer**: two to four plain sentences built from the same figures (the price against usual, the share of the month and days of limit, what he paid before when there are two or more, the verdict as need or want).

## Look
The exchanger's sheet (pixel title, glass). The item box shows the kind's symbol and a chip ("Shirt", "roughly a meal"); the price box takes 500.000, 500rb, 1,5jt or 500k. The verdict card is washed in the verdict's colour: the score in a ring (pixel type, counting) beside the word and the sentences. Then the price on a strip (log scale, so 50× fits): the usual range as a green-to-yellow band, the typical price as a tick, his as a dot; the month's budget as a bar; three tiles (each use, days of limit, what he usually pays). Each piece comes in once; changes ease (transform, the ring's dash). Reduce motion: fades.

- **Where**: the sidebar's tools (since that day a row of five symbols under Log, Worth it? first): the sixth, so it stands on a row of its own right under Worth it? (`celebration`). **⌘J** opens and shuts it (`KB_LIST` "weekend", changeable in Settings › Keyboard).
- **The idea**, from the usual advice found that day (a weekend cap set in advance, a fixed weekday allowance, what isn't spent rolling into the weekend): the week (Monday first) has a budget, its seven daily limits (`limitOf`).
